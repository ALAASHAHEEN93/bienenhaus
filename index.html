From hive to jar

How we make it

Three steps, all done by hand in our own honey house.

1

Harvest with the bloom

Colonies move with the flowers, from spring rapeseed to late-summer forest. We only take what the bees can spare.

2

Cold extraction

Frames are spun at room temperature and strained once through linen. No heating and no fine filtering.

3

Jarred by hand

Every jar is filled, sealed and labelled with its harvest date and lot number.

We are a family beekeeping business from Brandenburg. Since 2011 we have moved our colonies with the bloom, from rapeseed fields in May to spruce forests in August.

Why we keep bees

It started with one colony in the garden and a lot of questions. Today beekeeping is our job, but the idea is the same: take only what the bees can spare and let every harvest taste of the place it comes from.

That is why our honey is never heated, never blended with imported honey and never fine-filtered. Every lid carries a lot number, so you can trace your jar back to its hive and harvest week.

“A good honey tastes like one summer in one place. Our job is not to get in the way.”The beekeepers at Bienenhaus

A year at the hives

Spring

Waking up

First flights in March. We check every colony and move the first hives to the rapeseed fields.

Summer

Harvest

Acacia, wildflower, lavender and forest. Up to five harvests, each extracted and jarred separately.

Autumn

Giving back

The bees keep their own honey for winter. We treat for varroa with organic acids only.

Winter

Quiet season

The colonies cluster and rest. We repair frames, plan the season and pack your orders.
